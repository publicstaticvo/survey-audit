# Underlying Failure Taxonomy

Version 0.1 is an initial working classification. Cross-disciplinary validation is pending. Classify the defect by what fails, using any applicable combination of categories. Keep observations that do not fit in an extensible local namespace with a definition and an evidence anchor.

| Category | Definition and common failure modes | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem Framing Failure | The research question, motivation, target construct, or relevance is poorly specified or unjustified. Examples include a question that cannot be answered as posed and a premise that misstates the problem. | Contribution positioning concerns the novelty and relationship of the proposed answer to earlier answers. |
| Contribution Positioning Failure | The paper fails to establish what it adds and in what relation to relevant prior work. Examples include an indistinguishable contribution and a false priority claim. | Evidence failure concerns the grounds for a claim. Attribute both when missing grounds misrepresent priority. |
| Conceptual / Theoretical Failure | Scientific concepts, definitions, assumptions, models, or formal propositions fail within the stated theory. Examples include incoherent definitions and an invalid theorem. | Inferential failure concerns the step from grounds to a conclusion. A broken proof can warrant both categories. |
| Methodological Failure | Sampling, measurement, search, design, comparison, or analysis procedures prevent the study from answering its question. Examples include a biased inclusion rule and an invalid comparator. | Evidence failure concerns the observations available. An unsound measurement and the resulting weak observations can coexist. |
| Evidence Failure | Evidence needed by a substantive claim is missing, irrelevant, weak, or contradicted. Examples include an unsupported numerical assertion and a citation that addresses a different outcome. | Inferential failure concerns reasoning over the available observations. Integrity / Verifiability concerns establishing their identity, origin, or accessibility. |
| Inferential Failure | The reasoning connecting premises or evidence to a conclusion is invalid or insufficiently justified. Examples include treating association as causation and synthesizing incomparable studies as equivalent. | Scope / Generalization concerns transfer beyond the population or conditions actually supported. |
| Scope / Generalization Failure | The claimed population, period, domain, or applicability exceeds the justified boundary, or the stated scope excludes necessary evidence without justification. Examples include cross-population extrapolation and a material omission within scope. | Methodological failure concerns how evidence was obtained. A narrow sample and broad extrapolation may warrant both. |
| Integrity / Verifiability Failure | A material source, artifact, or provenance chain cannot be authenticated, traced, or inspected as represented. Examples include a misidentified reference and unavailable inputs essential for reproducing a result. | Record ordinary access limitations as uncertainty unless the evidence establishes a material verification failure. Intent requires independent evidence. |

## Communication / Presentation

Record presentation separately when organization, terminology, figures, tables, or exposition obstruct interpretation or use. Give the location and consequence. Add an underlying failure category when the same issue also changes a substantive scientific proposition.

## Classification record

A **Reviewer Concern** is a question or criticism someone raises. An **Underlying Failure** is the defect mechanism the audit investigates. An **Evidence-backed Finding** is a bounded proposition, with evidence and reasoning, about what was established. A concern may lead to several findings, a finding may bear several failure labels, and an investigation may end unresolved. Add, revise, or merge categories through the contribution process with neighboring examples and stable aliases for prior IDs.
